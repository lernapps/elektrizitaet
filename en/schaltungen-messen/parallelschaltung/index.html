Parallel circuit with meters

On this page

  1. The idea

The idea

Two resistors sit side by side on a battery, each in a branch of its own. You measure the current in the main wire and in one branch. The current splits at the junction, and both branches have the full voltage. You already know how a parallel circuit is built.

What to remember

In a parallel circuit: - Every branch has the same voltage: U=U1=U2. - The branch currents add up to the current in the main wire: I=I1+I2. - Each branch current is I1=UR1 and I2=UR2. - The total resistance is smaller than each single resistance, because the current has more paths.

The smaller resistor draws more current

Because every branch has the same voltage, the larger current flows in the branch with the smaller resistance.

Example

Two branches on 12 V

A resistor of 20 Ω and one of 30 Ω are connected in parallel on 12 V. Branch 1: I1=1220=0.6 A. Branch 2: I2=1230=0.4 A. The ammeter in the main wire shows 0.6+0.4=1.0 A.

Practice

Task: Two resistors of 10 Ω and 30 Ω are connected in parallel on 9 V. What does the ammeter show in the branch with the second resistor (30 Ω)?

Write down your prediction and check it: then the circuit is switched on and the meter shows the value.

This exercise shows two resistors in parallel on a battery. You predict what the meter shows, check, and the circuit switches on. Example: 12 V, 20 Ω and 30 Ω. Solution: 0.6 A in the first branch, 0.4 A in the second, 1.0 A together in the main wire.

Three worked tasks, each with its solution:

  1. Two resistors of 10 Ω and 30 Ω are connected in parallel on 6 V. What is the current in the main wire in amperes?

    Hint

    First calculate each branch current, then add.

    Solution
    1. I1=UR1, I2=UR2
    2. I=I1+I2=610+630=0.8 A

    Right answer: 0.8 A

  2. Two resistors of 30 Ω and 10 Ω are connected in parallel on 12 V. What is the current in the main wire in amperes?

    Hint

    First calculate each branch current, then add.

    Solution
    1. I1=UR1, I2=UR2
    2. I=I1+I2=1230+1210=1.6 A

    Right answer: 1.6 A

  3. Two resistors of 10 Ω and 60 Ω are connected in parallel on 12 V. What is the current in the main wire in amperes?

    Hint

    First calculate each branch current, then add.

    Solution
    1. I1=UR1, I2=UR2
    2. I=I1+I2=1210+1260=1.4 A

    Right answer: 1.4 A

Three worked tasks, each with its solution:

  1. In the main wire of a parallel circuit 0.6 A flows. In the first branch the ammeter shows 0.4 A. What is the current in the second branch in amperes?

    Hint

    I=I1+I2, so I2=I−I1.

    Solution
    1. I2=I−I1=0.6 A−0.4 A=0.2 A

    Right answer: 0.2 A

  2. In the main wire of a parallel circuit 0.8 A flows. In the first branch the ammeter shows 0.3 A. What is the current in the second branch in amperes?

    Hint

    I=I1+I2, so I2=I−I1.

    Solution
    1. I2=I−I1=0.8 A−0.3 A=0.5 A

    Right answer: 0.5 A

  3. In the main wire of a parallel circuit 1 A flows. In the first branch the ammeter shows 0.4 A. What is the current in the second branch in amperes?

    Hint

    I=I1+I2, so I2=I−I1.

    Solution
    1. I2=I−I1=1 A−0.4 A=0.6 A

    Right answer: 0.6 A

Two resistors are connected in parallel on 6 V. The voltmeter at the first resistor shows 6 V. What does it show at the second resistor?

  • 6 V right answer

    In a parallel circuit every branch has the full voltage of the source.

  • 3 V

    The voltage is only shared in a series circuit. Not in a parallel circuit.

  • 12 V

    The voltages do not add up. Both branches are across the same 6 V.

In a parallel circuit the voltage is the same across all branches.
