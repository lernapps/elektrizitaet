Circuit builder with meters

The idea

Now you build circuits that reach certain values. You get a task with a voltage and a target value, put resistors on the places of the circuit board and build in meters. The numbers change with every new task. The builder calculates what your circuit shows.

What to remember

  • The circuit board has four places: places 1 and 4 are in the main wire, places 2 and 3 are the two branches of a parallel circuit.
  • You build an ammeter in in series: it shows the current through the place.
  • You connect a voltmeter in parallel: it shows the voltage across the place.
  • An empty place is just a wire. A branch without a resistor bypasses the other one. With no resistor in the whole circuit there is a short circuit.

What the builder simplifies

The source and the meters are ideal: they have no resistance of their own. Direct current flows. A lamp would also have a fixed resistance here, which is why you use resistors.

Example

A current of 0.4 A on 12 V

The source has 12 V, the ammeter should show 0.4 A. The resistance must be R=UI=12 V0.4 A=30 Ω. Put a resistor of 30 Ω on place 1 and build the ammeter in there. It also works with 10 Ω and 20 Ω in series, because 10+20=30.

Practice

Task: The source has 9 V. Build a circuit in which a voltmeter across a resistor shows 2.25 V.

Tap a place to put in a resistor with the chosen value.

Short circuit! There is no resistor in the circuit, and the current would be far too large. The meters show nothing.

Solution: 20 Ω on place 2 and 30 Ω on place 3, ammeter at place 1.

You put a resistor into place 2 and leave place 3 empty. Places 1 and 4 are empty. What happens?

  • There is a short circuit: the empty branch is just a wire, and there is no resistor in the circuit. right answer

    The current takes the wire and bypasses the resistor. The circuit then has no resistor to limit the current.

  • The current flows through the resistor, the ammeter shows U divided by R.

    The current takes the path without a resistor, not the one with the resistor.

  • Nothing happens.

    An empty branch changes the circuit a lot: it bypasses the other branch.
