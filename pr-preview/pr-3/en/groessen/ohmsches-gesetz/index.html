The idea

You connect a resistor to a battery and measure. With twice the voltage, twice as much current flows. The ratio of voltage to current stays the same: it is the resistance. This relation is Ohm's law.

What to remember

Ohm's law

For an ohmic resistor the voltage divided by the current is always the same: R=UI. The resistance does not change when you change the voltage.

R=UI
U=R⋅I
I=UR

The unit: 1 Ω = 1 V : 1 A. Convert the current to amperes first, not milliamperes.

Characteristic curve

A characteristic curve shows how the current depends on the voltage. For an ohmic resistor it is a straight line through the origin.

Not every part is ohmic

A bulb gets hot when it shines, and its resistance grows. Its characteristic curve is therefore bent, not a straight line. R=UI still holds for it at every single moment, but the value changes.

Example

A resistor on the battery

A resistor of 20 Ω is connected to 6 V. We want the current: I=UR=6 V20 Ω=0.3 A. The ammeter shows 0.3 A, that is 300 mA.

Practice

Three worked tasks, each with its solution:

  1. A resistor of 20 Ω is connected to 3 V. What is the current in amperes?

    Hint

    I=UR

    Solution
    1. I=UR
    2. I=3 V20 Ω=0.15 A

    Right answer: 0.15 A

  2. A resistor of 100 Ω is connected to 3 V. What is the current in amperes?

    Hint

    I=UR

    Solution
    1. I=UR
    2. I=3 V100 Ω=0.03 A

    Right answer: 0.03 A

  3. A resistor of 20 Ω is connected to 9 V. What is the current in amperes?

    Hint

    I=UR

    Solution

    2. I=9 V20 Ω=0.45 A

    Right answer: 0.45 A

You double the voltage across an ohmic resistor. Then the current doubles too.

Right answer: The statement is true.

Because the ratio $\frac{U}{I}$ stays the same, the current grows in step with the voltage. The characteristic curve is a straight line through the origin.
